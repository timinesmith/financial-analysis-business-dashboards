# Financial Analysis & Business Dashboards

## Executive summary

A portfolio of financial-control and reporting demonstrations: budget variance tracking, event-level P&L structure, cash-flow forecasting, KPI definitions, and reconciliation controls.

## Business problem

Operators often receive accounting totals too late to influence staffing, purchasing, vendor commitments, or event decisions. A useful reporting system connects budgets, actual results, open commitments, and explanations while the work is still underway.

## Scope

- Budget and variance tracking with synthetic data
- Event-level P&L framework
- Thirteen-week-style cash-flow forecast structure presented as a six-month example
- KPI dashboard specification
- Review and escalation controls

## Role and evidence basis

**Illustrative demonstration.** All numbers in this repository are synthetic. The framework design is informed by Timothy Nesmith's venue finance and administration experience, including a centralized accounts-payable register, event-level P&L reporting framework, and structured receivables follow-up process.

No employer ledgers, invoices, payroll records, bank data, or internal reports are included.

## Methods and tools

Microsoft Excel, financial reporting, budget management, variance analysis, accounts payable/receivable processes, reconciliations, KPI design, and dashboard specification. Relevant business systems documented in his experience include Paycor, Toast POS, Compeat Advantage, Tipalti, and QuickBooks.

## Verified experience outcomes

- Supported financial and administrative execution for 150+ events in approximately five months.
- Implemented three financial-control processes: centralized AP register, event P&L framework, and structured AR follow-up.
- Financial analysis identified approximately $6 million in grant allocation discrepancies and escalated them to leadership for review and reconciliation. This is not represented as recovered funds.

## Skills demonstrated

Financial operations, budgeting, forecasting, variance analysis, reporting design, reconciliations, vendor management, and process improvement.

## How clients benefit

Clients gain a reporting rhythm that shows where money is committed, where performance differs from plan, which variances need explanation, and who owns the next corrective action.
